#30DayMapChallenge · Ring forts

Ireland’s ring forts and the Ordnance Survey

17,007 ring forts are mapped in OpenStreetMap, most of them derived from Ordnance Survey maps.

For the official record of every known fort in the Republic, use the National Monuments Service’s Historic Environment Viewer.

Background

What the fort’s source tag says

  • British War Office map
  • Ordnance Survey 1st/2nd edition
  • Aerial imagery, National Monuments Service, survey, books
  • No source tag
Where the data comes from

How most of these forts got into OpenStreetMap:

  1. 1829–42. The Ordnance Survey maps Ireland at six inches to the mile, forts included. About 4,700 forts here were derived from its 1st or 2nd edition.
  2. From 1940. The War Office shrinks the c.1910 sheets to 1:25,000 (GSGS 3906).
  3. 2010s. Trinity College Dublin gives its copies to OSM Ireland. Volunteers line them up on mapwarper.net to map townlands, and trace forts along the way: about 5,300 here.
  4. 2021 → 2026. About 5,000 forts mapped (source), then 17,007 by 6 Nov 2026.

The official record is the Sites and Monuments Record, now open data. Forts that carry its number link to their entry.
